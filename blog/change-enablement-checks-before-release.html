Change enablement: the checks before a release goes live

1 min read

Change enablement is the practice of checking a proposed change before it reaches production. The goal is not to slow work down. It is to catch the change that would break a service at the worst possible time. A good change request answers four questions: what changes, what could go wrong, how it was tested, and how it gets undone.

The checks reviewers actually apply

  • Impact: which services and users are affected, including downstream systems the requester may not know about.
  • Testing: what was tested, in which environment, and what the results showed, not just a claim that testing happened.
  • Timing: whether the window avoids peak hours, freezes, and other changes to the same service.
  • Rollback: a concrete backout plan with a named decision maker, not a vague promise to fix forward.

When writing a change request, imagine the reviewer knows nothing about your project. If they can approve it from the request alone, it is written well.
