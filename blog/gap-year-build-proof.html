Career Change

Using a gap year to build proof, not just certificates

1 min read

A gap year is often spent collecting certificates that prove attendance, not ability. Employers care about the second. A year spent building proof, such as projects, freelance work, or documented practice, ends with something to show in interviews. Certificates help only when they sit on top of real work.

Making the year count

  • Pick one target field and one outcome for the year, stated plainly, such as three finished projects.
  • Work in public where possible: write-ups, code, or case notes that others can read and judge.
  • Seek one real client or volunteer project, since real constraints teach what tutorials cannot.
  • Review monthly against the outcome, and drop activities that do not serve it.

At the end of the year, you should be able to show your work and explain your decisions. If you can only list courses, the year did not do its job.
