Month-end close checklists that prevent last-day panic

1 min read

Month-end close compresses a month of accounting into a few days, and the panic comes from tasks discovered late. A good checklist spreads the work: some tasks happen weekly, some on the last working day, and some only after the books close. The checklist itself matters less than the discipline of following the same sequence every month.

What belongs on the checklist

  • Recurring journals posted early, such as rent and depreciation, instead of all at once on the last day.
  • Bank reconciliations completed for every account, with breaks assigned, not just noted.
  • Accruals reviewed for completeness, including expenses incurred but not yet invoiced.
  • A final review step where a second person checks the trial balance before the close is declared.

After each close, note what went wrong and add one step to prevent it next month. Checklists improve by use, not by rewriting.
