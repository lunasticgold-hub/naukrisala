AML & KYC

PEPs and why family members count in screening

1 min read

A politically exposed person, or PEP, is someone with a prominent public role, such as a minister, judge, or senior executive of a state-owned company. Their family members and close associates are screened as well. The reason is practical: someone who wants to move illicit funds through a public official will often use a relative or associate as the account holder instead.

Who counts as connected

  • Immediate family: spouse, partner, children, parents, and siblings, depending on the policy in use.
  • Close associates: business partners and people with joint accounts or shared ownership of companies.
  • Former officials: many policies keep PEP status for a period after the person leaves office.

Being connected to a PEP is not an accusation. It means the account gets deeper checks and more frequent review. Analysts should explain this calmly to customers, because most connected people are ordinary account holders with an ordinary need for banking.
